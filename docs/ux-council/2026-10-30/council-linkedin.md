# Council note: what LinkedIn can teach Sawwiq

Platform-business specialist. 30 Oct 2026. Research only; no repo files changed.

## The short answer

LinkedIn is a good teacher, but mostly for **order and patience**, not for copying features.

- It took LinkedIn about 2 years to charge anyone, 3 years to turn a profit, 5 years to sell ads and 8 years to IPO.
- Its biggest money never came from members paying for themselves. It came from **the side with the budget** (recruiters, then advertisers, then sales teams) paying to reach members.
- Its one freelancer marketplace (ProFinder, now "Services Marketplace") is the closest thing to Sawwiq, and it is its **weakest** product. It ended automatic matching in May 2024.
- Jordan has hundreds of agencies, not a billion members. LinkedIn's "my network grows my value" effect does not exist here. Sawwiq's value comes from the other side: **requests that get good quotes fast**.

So: copy LinkedIn's profile-completion engine, its public pages for search, and its revenue sequence. Do not copy its invite spam, its endorsements, its "who viewed you" paywall, or its pay-to-reply marketplace.

## 1. How LinkedIn started (2002 to 2008)

**Dates.**
- Founded 14 Dec 2002 by Reid Hoffman and friends. Went live 5 May 2003 when the founders emailed invitations. ([Timeline of LinkedIn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_LinkedIn))
- 1 million members by Aug 2004. First profitable month March 2006. 10 million by April 2007. ([Wikipedia: LinkedIn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LinkedIn))

**The cold start.**
- Growth was slow. About 4,500 members in month one, some days only 20 sign-ups. ([Product Stories](https://productstories.substack.com/p/linkedin-and-email-invitations))
- Hoffman personally invited well-connected people. That made the brand look serious, but it did not make it grow.
- Profiles were not public at first, so search engines could not send anyone.

**What actually grew it.**
- **Address-book upload and invitations (2004).** Members uploaded contacts and invited them. Invites expired after two weeks, which pushed people to accept. An Outlook uploader gave 5 to 10 times more invites per person than webmail, because Outlook users were exactly LinkedIn's audience. ([Product Stories](https://productstories.substack.com/p/linkedin-and-email-invitations))
- **Public profiles (2006).** Opening profiles to Google became the second big growth channel. A member's own name search led to their LinkedIn page. (Aatif Awan, LinkedIn VP Growth, [summary in search results](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/building-a-community-of-half-a-billion-by-aatif-awan-vp-growth-linkedin/76677955); slides did not load for me, so treat this as secondary.)
- **The hook: profile as résumé.** You built it once for yourself, and it kept working for you while you slept. Nobody had to be "active" for it to be useful.

**Early revenue, in order.**
| When | What | Who paid |
|---|---|---|
| Jul–Aug 2005 | Premium accounts ("Business Accounts", better search) | Power users |
| 2005 | Job listings | Employers |
| May 2007 | "Who's viewed your profile" (free teaser, full list paid) | Members |
| Sep 2008 | Targeted ads using profile data | Advertisers |

Sources: [Timeline of LinkedIn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_LinkedIn). The 2005 jobs date is widely repeated but I could not confirm it in a primary source.

**The mix shifted toward the buyer side.** In 2007, Premium subscriptions were 53% of revenue and hiring was 23%. By 2010 hiring was 42% ($101.8M), marketing 33% ($79.3M) and Premium only 25% ($61.9M) of $243M. ([TechCrunch on the S-1](https://techcrunch.com/?p=283889), [Renaissance Capital](https://www.renaissancecapital.com/IPO-Center/News/8967/LinkedIn-files-much-anticipated-S-1))

> Lesson: members paying for themselves got LinkedIn started. Buyers paying to reach members made it big.

## 2. How it grew and what it became

- **IPO:** 19 May 2011 at $45, about $4.3B valuation; closed day one at $94.25, about $9B. ([CNN Money](https://money.cnn.com/2011/05/19/technology/linkedin_IPO/?iid=EL), [AP via Washington Times](https://m.washingtontimes.com/news/2011/may/18/linkedins-ipo-priced-at-45-a-share/))
- **Big buys:** SlideShare 2012 ($119M), Lynda.com 2015 ($1.5B, became LinkedIn Learning in 2016). ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LinkedIn))
- **Microsoft:** announced June 2016, closed 8 Dec 2016, $26.2B. ([Timeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_LinkedIn))
- **Today:** about 1.3B registered members (early 2026). FY2026 revenue (year to 30 June 2026) about **$19.8B**, up 11% (12% in Q4). ([Microsoft FY26 Q4 metrics](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/Investor/earnings/FY-2026-Q4/metrics), [Wikipedia infobox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LinkedIn)). Microsoft does not publish the split by line any more.

**Revenue lines (latest public markers).**
| Line | What it sells | Latest figure found |
|---|---|---|
| Talent Solutions (Recruiter, job ads) | Employers pay to find and reach members | Passed $6B trailing 12 months in FY22 ([LinkedIn news](https://news.linkedin.com:443/2022/july/linkedin-business-highlights-from-microsoft-s-fy22-q4-earnings)); now under pressure from weak hiring |
| Marketing Solutions (ads) | Advertisers pay to reach members | Passed $5B a year in FY22 (same source) |
| Premium subscriptions | Members pay for insight and reach | Passed $2B a year in Jan 2025 ([LinkedIn news](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/linkedin-business-highlights-from-microsoft-s-q2-fy25-earnings), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/29/linkedin-passes-2b-in-premium-revenues-in-12-months-with-overall-revenues-up-9-on-the-year)) |
| Sales Navigator (Sales Solutions) | Sales teams pay to find buyers | Called the fastest-growing part of Premium in 2015 ([LinkedIn Q4 2015](https://news.linkedin.com/2016/linkedin-announces-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2015-results)) |
| Learning | Courses for members and companies | Built on Lynda.com |

Third-party splits for 2024 disagree wildly (Marketing at 28% or 41%), so I do not quote them.

### ProFinder / Services Marketplace: the closest cousin

- **Launched 2015–2016** as ProFinder. A buyer answered a few questions and got **up to 5 proposals** from freelancers. About 60,000 freelancers in 140+ service areas by 2017. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LinkedIn), [LinkedIn ProFinder page](https://business.linkedin.com/grow/profinder))
- **Fees:** free for buyers. Freelancers got a few free proposals (sources say 5, later 10), then had to pay for **Premium Business, about $60 a month**. No payments, no escrow, no project tools. ([Motley Fool 2016](https://www.fool.com/investing/general/2016/05/10/can-linkedin-take-on-this-715-billion-market.aspx), [Millo](https://millo.co/linkedin-profinder), [BetterTeam](https://betterteam.com/linkedin-profinder))
- **Rebranded** to Services Marketplace with "Service Pages" in 2021.
- **May 2024:** LinkedIn **ended matching** of requests to former ProFinder providers. Now a buyer can optionally send a request to more providers, and **only Premium Business subscribers can answer**. ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a564588))

What this tells Sawwiq:
- A services marketplace bolted onto a network **does not run itself**. LinkedIn had the traffic and still pulled back from matching.
- Charging the provider to **reply** turns the marketplace into a lead-selling business. Buyers then see only the providers who paid, not the best ones. That is close to pay-for-relevance.
- LinkedIn never handled the money, so it never earned from the outcome. Sawwiq's protected contracts are the part LinkedIn **did not** build. That is Sawwiq's edge, not a gap.

## 3. What drove profile completion

**The meter.** LinkedIn showed a progress bar, later named levels (Beginner, Intermediate, All-Star). It deliberately showed progress without a hard number, so people felt close to done. ([pettauer.net](https://pettauer.net/en/linkedins-all-star-profile-progress-bar/), [LinkedIn Help: profile level meter](https://www.linkedin.com/help/learning/answer/391))
- LinkedIn tied completeness to **search appearances**: complete profiles show up more. That is the honest reason to finish.
- In 2012 it added **freshness**: recently updated profiles score higher. ([search summary of LinkedIn blog, Feb 2012](https://blog.linkedin.com/2012/02/14/profile-completeness))
- "Guided edit" asked for one thing at a time, in a fixed order; a patent proposed reordering tasks per person to get more done. ([Justia patent 20160055010](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20160055010))
- Jeff Weiner (2014 earnings call): profiles with a photo get **14 times more views**. ([Benzinga call highlights](https://feeds2.benzinga.com/news/earnings/14/05/4522955/linkedin-conference-call-highlights))
- The often-quoted "progress bar raised completion by 20%" has **no primary source** I could find. Do not repeat it.

**Importing.** Résumé and address-book import meant nobody started from a blank page. Sawwiq already has the equivalent: PDF import (docs/36) and Behance import (docs/47).

**Endorsements (Sept 2012).** One-click "endorse for a skill". It made profiles look fuller and created lots of notifications, but it was widely mocked: strangers endorsing skills they never saw, and LinkedIn suggesting skills people did not have. LinkedIn later reworked it, and in 2024 dropped skill-test badges, saying hiring managers want **real examples of work**. ([Timeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_LinkedIn), [Decision Marketing](https://www.decisionmarketing.co.uk/news/linkedin-endorsements-under-fire), [Next Avenue](https://www.nextavenue.org/memo-linkedin-please-fix-endorsements/))

**"Who viewed your profile" (May 2007).** Free users see the last 5 viewers; Premium sees everyone for 90 days, with filters and trends. Widely called the top reason people pay, though LinkedIn never published conversion numbers. ([LinkedIn Premium playbook PDF](https://premium.linkedin.com/content/dam/premium/jobsearch/PDFAssets/JSS_Playbook.pdf), [LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/learning/answer/a516745))

**"People also viewed" / "People you may know".** Endless side-loops that keep people browsing and connecting. No public numbers found.

## 4. What failed or was criticised

| Problem | What happened | Sawwiq rule it touches |
|---|---|---|
| Spam invites | *Perkins v. LinkedIn*: reminder emails sent to people's contacts without proper consent. **$13M settlement** (approved Feb 2016). The first invite had consent; the two reminders did not. ([Techlicious](https://www.techlicious.com/blog/linkedin-to-pay-13-million-settlement-for-add-connections-service-spam/), [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/web/linkedin-pays-13m-to-settle-suit-regarding-excessive-emailing-tells-users-via-email/)) | PDPL consent; never log or misuse emails/phones |
| ProFinder | Pay-to-reply, thin leads, matching shut in 2024 (above) | Pay never buys relevance |
| Fake profiles | Fake accounts used for spying and phishing; LinkedIn was the most impersonated brand in phishing in 2022. It now blocks most fakes at sign-up (16.4M stopped at registration in H1 2022). ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LinkedIn), [LinkedIn transparency H1 2022](https://news.linkedin.com/2022/december/transparency-report--first-half-of-2022)) | Verified badge, KYC before money |
| Engagement bait | "Comment YES if you agree", engagement pods, polls. LinkedIn now down-ranks them. ([LinkedIn newsroom 2026](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/ImprovingTheFeed), [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/linkedin-feed-less-polls-engagement-bait-384986)) | Feed (docs/38) must reward real work |
| Data scraping | hiQ case; claims of 500–700M scraped records (2021) | Agency contact data, signed URLs |
| Endorsements | Seen as meaningless (above) | Reviews only from verified clients |

## 5. What Sawwiq should take, for agencies

What Sawwiq already has that maps to LinkedIn:
- Studio setup checklist and pioneer medal checklist (docs/57) ≈ the progress meter.
- PDF and Behance import (docs/36, 47) ≈ résumé import.
- Studio → Insights: views, contact clicks, times recommended ≈ "who viewed you".
- Hire pages per service and city, SEO rules (docs/18) ≈ public profiles.
- Verified reviews (Stage 9) ≈ recommendations, done better than endorsements.
- Referral agents with an "active" rule (docs/42) ≈ the invite engine, paid by results.
- Founder Advantage (docs/44): 100 seats, 0% first project, 7% first year, 6 months Pro ≈ LinkedIn's early-adopter goodwill.
- Owner matching (docs/59), requests and quotes (docs/12) ≈ ProFinder, but with consent and anonymity.

### Why network effects differ here

- LinkedIn's value grew with **same-side** connections: more colleagues, more value. Agencies in Amman do not want to "connect" with competitors.
- Sawwiq's value is **cross-side**: more good agencies make clients come; more client requests make agencies stay.
- With a few hundred agencies, the network is **dense, not big**. Every agency is one or two people away from every other. Word of mouth and reputation move fast, both good and bad. One spam incident spreads to all of them.
- So the north-star number is not sign-ups. It is: **share of requests that get 3 or more relevant quotes within 48 hours**, and **share of agencies that got at least one real request this month**.

### Lessons, applied

**L1. Page strength meter with an honest reason (from the progress bar).**
- Turn the setup checklist into a permanent "page strength" meter in Studio with 3 levels (for example: Started, Ready, Strong). Arabic first.
- Each step names its real effect, using rules that already exist: "Without services you cannot appear in matches" (matcher excludes you); "Each tagged work post adds to your match score" (portfolio is 15 of 100 points); "A starting price lets us show you to clients with a budget" (budget fit is 15 points).
- Add freshness: a nudge when no new work in 60 days.
- One step at a time ("Next: add a package"), not a long list.
- Do NOT: let the meter or level change ranking by itself, sell levels, or show "complete" to clients as if it meant quality.
- Impact high, effort low. Safe.

**L2. Import first, type later (from résumé and address-book import).**
- Keep Behance and PDF import as the first screen after joining. Add Google Business Profile pull for logo, hours and rating where already connected.
- Consider Instagram import later; the Instagram API needs a business account and app review, so start with "paste 6 post links".
- Impact high, effort low (mostly done). Safe.

**L3. Public pages that rank and travel (from public profiles, 2006).**
- Make each agency page and each indexable post the best Arabic result for "[agency name]" and "[service] [city]".
- Give every agency a "See our work on Sawwiq" link and badge for its Instagram bio, website and WhatsApp Business catalogue. `share-card.tsx` is a start. Every agency's own audience becomes Sawwiq traffic, like a person's name search did for LinkedIn.
- Impact high, effort low. Safe.

**L4. Paid insight, not paid exposure (from "who viewed your profile").**
- Do NOT show agencies which visitor or which business looked at them. Visitors are anonymous by design, and briefs are anonymised until a quote. Exposing that breaks the rules and PDPL.
- Instead sell **aggregate** insight in Pro, already in the docs/10 plan: 12 months of history; "you were recommended 14 times this month for Instagram ads in Amman"; "requests you could have quoted on but missed"; "your starting price vs the market range". Free plan keeps 30 days.
- This is LinkedIn's best Premium hook, made privacy-safe.
- Impact high (it is the Pro reason to pay), effort medium. Turn on only with `MONETIZATION_ENABLED` after the docs/10 triggers.

**L5. Charge the side with the budget, later (from LinkedIn's revenue mix).**
- LinkedIn started with member subscriptions (53% in 2007) and grew on buyer-side products (hiring, ads).
- For Sawwiq the natural order is: free now → Pro for agencies (docs/10 triggers) → **commission on protected contracts** (the founder 7% rate, docs/44) → later a **buyer-side tool** for big buyers.
- Big buyers in Jordan exist: banks, telecoms, universities, and many NGOs and UN agencies in Amman that run formal tenders for communications work. A "Sawwiq for teams" plan (multi-quote tender, comparison, contract, escrow, invoices) is Sawwiq's "Recruiter". The buyer pays for workflow, never for ranking.
- Impact very high long term, effort high. Year 2. Payments rule applies.

**L6. Do not copy ProFinder's pay-to-reply (from Services Marketplace).**
- The docs/10 free limit of 5 quotes a month is fine as a fairness cap. Do not make paid plans the only way to answer requests, and do not sell individual leads early (docs/10 stream 4 is already "only if").
- Keep the strengths LinkedIn lacked: anonymised briefs, capped quotes per request (10), relevance-only invitations, and protected payment.
- Watch quality: if agencies quote on everything, cap quotes per agency per week, not by price.
- Impact high (protects trust), effort low. This is a "don't".

**L7. Invites with consent, one reminder at most (from the $13M lawsuit).**
- Agencies may invite their **clients to review** them and their **freelance partners** to join (collaboration, docs/40). One message the agency sends itself (WhatsApp share link is best in Jordan), no automatic follow-ups from Sawwiq.
- Never upload or store an agency's contact book. Never email people who did not ask.
- Referral agents (docs/42) are the paid version of LinkedIn's invites and already only pay for **active** pages. Keep that rule; audit for fake sign-ups.
- Impact medium, effort low. Safe if kept consent-based.

**L8. Proof, not endorsements.**
- Do not add one-click endorsements or "likes for skills". In a small market they become favour-trading between friends.
- Do add **client-confirmed work**: the agency tags a portfolio client (docs/28); Sawwiq asks the client one question by link: "Did this agency do this work for you?" A yes adds a "Confirmed by client" mark to the post. It is a light version of verified reviews and much harder to fake.
- Partner credits: when two agencies collaborate on a contract (docs/40), both pages can show "worked with" after both agree.
- Impact medium-high, effort medium. Must keep client contact data out of the AI and logs.

**L9. "Similar agencies", carefully (from "people also viewed").**
- Clients benefit from "Other agencies for Instagram ads in Amman" after they leave a profile without contacting.
- But agencies hate seeing competitors on their own page, and docs/10 already forbids promoting another agency there. Show it only on exit (back from profile, or after the match chat), organic only, never sponsored.
- Do NOT sell "remove competitors from my page". It is pay-to-change-what-clients-see. Owner decision if ever.
- Impact medium, effort low.

**L10. Feed rewards work, not bait (from engagement bait).**
- In the feed (docs/38), rank by real work quality signals and freshness, not raw likes and comments. No "like to win" contests, no polls in the feed.
- Impact medium, effort low.

**L11. Verification and fake pages (from fake profiles).**
- Keep the verified badge meaningful: commercial registration or a real client review. KYC before any money moves (docs/44 already says so).
- Detect copied portfolios: the same image on two agency pages. Behance credit links help.
- Impact medium, effort medium.

**L12. Data as PR (from LinkedIn's workforce reports).**
- Publish a yearly "Social media prices in Jordan" report from real package prices (docs/10 stream 5). It gets press, ranks in search and is a reason for agencies to keep prices current.
- Impact medium, effort medium, Year 2 or when there are 100+ priced agencies.

**L13. Learning, much later.**
- LinkedIn Learning cost $1.5B and is a separate business. Skip. At most, short Arabic guides for business owners on how to brief an agency, which also improves request quality.

### What NOT to copy (summary)

- Address-book import and automatic invite reminders.
- Endorsements and skill badges.
- Showing who viewed a page, or any client identity before a quote.
- Pay-to-reply to requests; pay-per-lead before subscriptions stall.
- Ads before 20,000 monthly visitors (LinkedIn waited 5 years).
- Engagement-bait mechanics in the feed.
- Any plan perk that changes match relevance beyond the capped, labelled "Featured" boost.

## Flags against the non-negotiables

- **Breaks the rules if copied:** "who viewed your page" with names (anonymity, PDPL); pay-to-reply or paid early access beyond the relevance-gated founder window (pay for relevance); address-book upload (consent); sending client contacts to the AI for "smarter matching" (AI rule).
- **Allowed only after triggers:** Pro insight paywall, contract commission, promoted posts, buyer-side plan.
- **Arabic-first check:** every meter label, badge text and share card must exist in `messages/ar.json` and `en.json`, and be tested at 390px RTL.
