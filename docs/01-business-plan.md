# 01 · Business Plan — Sawwiq (سوّق)

*Working name. "Sawwiq" means "market it" in Arabic. Run a trademark search at the Ministry of Industry, Trade & Supply and check .jo / .com domain availability before committing.*

Prepared: September 2026 · Stage: pre-seed, pre-product · Market: Jordan, then Levant and Gulf

---

## 1. Executive summary

Sawwiq is a two-sided marketplace that connects Jordanian businesses with verified social media and digital marketing agencies. A business describes what it needs in a five-minute Arabic brief. Three to five verified agencies respond with standardised proposals. The business compares, hires, and pays through the platform.

**The problem.** Jordan has roughly 97,000 SMEs and a social media ad market of USD 66 M growing 11% a year, but no local, trusted way to find and hire an agency. Businesses rely on word of mouth and Instagram DMs. Agencies rely on the same. Global directories (Clutch, Sortlist, DesignRush) list a few dozen Jordanian agencies in English for foreign buyers, and the only regional RFQ marketplace (Entasher) has eight Jordanian agencies and no local presence.

**The product.** An Arabic-first, mobile-first marketplace with three layers built in order: (1) a verified agency directory with structured service packages, (2) a brief-and-proposal flow with routed leads, (3) contracts and escrow payments so that the transaction, not just the introduction, happens on Sawwiq.

**The business model.** Free for businesses. Agencies pay per qualified lead (15 JOD), a Pro subscription for placement and unlimited responses (49–59 JOD/month), and an 8% commission on projects paid through escrow. Year 3 revenue target: ~160,000 JOD with ~2,600 briefs/year and ~85 paying agencies.

**The ask.** 40,000 JOD (≈ USD 56,000) of pre-seed capital or founder funding covers 18 months to break-even on a monthly basis. The company is profitable in Year 3 on Jordan alone; the venture case is regional expansion to Iraq, Palestine and the Gulf with Jordanian supply.

**Why now.** PDPL is in force (a compliance moat against informal competitors), CliQ and eFAWATEERcom have made online B2B payment normal in Jordan, JEDCO is granting SMEs money for marketing, and no incumbent has moved.

## 2. Problem

**For the business owner** (restaurant, clinic, boutique, real-estate office, school, e-commerce store):
- No idea who the good agencies are or what a fair price is. Quotes for "social media management" range from 150 JOD to 2,000 JOD a month with no way to compare.
- Fear of being burned. Everyone has a story of an agency that took the retainer and posted three stock images.
- No recourse. There is no contract, no reviews, no one to complain to.
- Outside Amman, no options at all.

**For the agency:**
- Leads come from referrals and cold outreach. Feast or famine.
- Time wasted on unqualified enquiries with no budget.
- No way to prove credibility to a stranger except a portfolio PDF over WhatsApp.
- Global directories charge in dollars or euros for leads from abroad that rarely convert.

**For the market:**
- Roughly 3,500 qualified agency searches per year (est.) happen through DMs and phone calls. Nobody captures the demand, the pricing data, or the outcomes.

## 3. Solution and product

### 3.1 Core loop

1. **Business posts a brief.** Guided Arabic form: business type, goal, platforms, monthly budget band, city, timeline. No account needed until submission. Phone OTP verifies the submitter.
2. **Sawwiq qualifies the brief.** Automated rules plus a human check in Year 1: real business, real budget, real intent. Unqualified briefs are answered with a self-serve resource, not routed.
3. **Routed to 3–5 matching verified agencies.** Match on service, budget band, industry experience, city, and response history. Agencies get a WhatsApp and email notification.
4. **Agencies respond with a standard proposal.** Fixed template: scope, deliverables, monthly price, timeline, team, two relevant case studies. Response time is tracked and public.
5. **Business compares and hires.** Side-by-side proposals, verified reviews, in-platform chat.
6. **Contract and payment on platform** (Phase 3). Standard Arabic/English agreement, milestone escrow through a licensed PSP, release on approval, dispute process.
7. **Review.** Only businesses that hired through the platform can review. Reviews feed matching.

### 3.2 What makes it defensible

- **Verification standard.** A "Sawwiq Verified" badge requires a Companies Control Department certificate, a live portfolio, and two contactable client references. Nobody in Jordan certifies agencies today.
- **Structured packages, not free text.** Agencies list fixed offers ("Instagram management, 12 posts + stories, 350 JOD/month"). Buyers decide faster, and Sawwiq accumulates the only real pricing dataset in the country.
- **Transaction rail.** Escrow and standard contracts make it safer to transact through Sawwiq than around it. This is the only durable answer to leakage.
- **Local depth.** Arabic-first, city pages for Irbid, Zarqa, Aqaba, Salt, Madaba, Karak. Local phone support. Global players will not do this.

### 3.3 Phasing

| Phase | Months | What ships | Success metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 · Validate | 1–2 | Landing page, 20 SME + 15 agency interviews, 100 seeded agency profiles, waitlist | 30 agencies pre-committed to claim profiles; 50 SME waitlist |
| 1 · Directory + briefs | 3–6 | Public directory, claim & verify flow, brief form, manual routing by ops, WhatsApp notifications, admin panel | 40 verified agencies; 30 briefs/month; 70% brief response rate within 48h |
| 2 · Marketplace | 7–12 | Automated matching, proposal template, in-app chat, reviews, agency dashboard, lead fees + Pro subscription live | 60 briefs/month; 15 paying agencies; first 1,000 JOD revenue month |
| 3 · Transaction rail | 13–18 | Standard contract, escrow via PSP, milestone release, disputes, agency payouts, city SEO pages | 25% of hires paid through platform; 40+ paying agencies |
| 4 · Expand | 19–36 | Adjacent categories (video, photo, web, branding), institutional demand partners, second market (Iraq or KSA secondary cities) | Regional revenue >20% |

- *Agencies build their own lead channels:* they already do, and it is not working for most. We are additive, not a replacement.

## 6. Business model

**Free for businesses, always.** Demand is the scarce side; never tax it.

| Stream | Mechanism | Price | Starts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead fee | Agency pays to submit a proposal on a qualified brief. Max 5 responders per brief. Refunded if the brief is withdrawn or found fake. | 15 JOD / response | Month 7 |
| Pro subscription | Featured placement in directory and city pages, unlimited responses (no lead fee), analytics, "Verified Pro" badge, priority routing | 49 JOD/mo (Y1–2), 59 JOD/mo (Y3) | Month 7 |
| Escrow commission | On projects contracted and paid through the platform | 8% of GMV (client pays 0%, agency absorbs) | Month 13 |
| Featured & sponsored | Homepage and category sponsorship, limited slots | 150–400 JOD/mo | Year 2 |
| Data and reports | Annual "State of Agency Pricing in Jordan" report, benchmark dashboards | 200–500 JOD | Year 2 |
| Institutional partnerships | Vetted-vendor programme for JEDCO grantees, bank SME arms, donor projects | Per-programme fee | Year 2 |

**Unit economics (Year 2 assumptions).** A qualified brief generates 3 paid responses × 15 JOD = 45 JOD. Cost to acquire a qualified brief via paid social at Jordan CPMs plus ops time: ~20–25 JOD (est.). A Pro subscriber pays 588 JOD/year; cost to acquire and serve an agency: ~150 JOD (est.). Escrow contributes 72 JOD per average 900 JOD project.

**Why agencies will pay.** A converted lead is worth 600–1,800 JOD/month in retainer. Paying 15 JOD per response with a realistic 1-in-5 close rate means 75 JOD per client won. Sortlist charges the same agencies €300/month plus per-lead fees for European leads that rarely convert.

## 7. Go-to-market

Full playbook in `07-go-to-market.md`. Summary:

**Supply first (Months 1–3).**
- Seed 100+ agency profiles from public sources. Agencies register to claim and control them.
- Founding Member offer to the first 40: lifetime free Pro, homepage feature at launch, input into the verification standard.
- One person, full time, on the phone and in agency offices in Amman. This is a sales job, not a marketing job.

**Demand (Months 3–6).**
- Paid Meta and Snapchat campaigns targeting business-page admins in Jordan. CPMs are under USD 2.50, so 1,200 JOD/month buys meaningful reach.
- Arabic content: "What should social media management cost in Jordan?" and city/industry pages for organic search, where nothing in Arabic exists today.
- Partnerships: JEDCO grant recipients, Amman Chamber of Commerce members, coworking spaces, bank SME programmes.
- Agencies themselves: every verified agency gets a "Get a quote via Sawwiq" badge and link for their own site and Instagram bio. Supply markets demand.

**Launch event.** A half-day "Agency Day" in Amman, Month 4, with founding agencies and 100 invited business owners. Cheap, and it creates the first briefs and first reviews on the same day.

## 8. Operations

**Year 1 team.**
- Founder: product, partnerships, fundraising, agency relationships.
- Ops & sales lead (Month 4): agency onboarding, verification, brief qualification, manual routing, support.
- Part-time content & design (Month 4): Arabic content, social, city pages.
- Engineering: founder with Claude Code, plus a contract developer for reviews, security and payments integration in Phase 3.

**Year 2.** Add a second ops/sales person and make the founder salaried. **Year 3.** Ops ×2, sales, support.

**Key processes to document from day one:** verification checklist, brief qualification rubric, routing rules, response-time SLAs for agencies (48h), dispute procedure, review moderation policy, data retention and deletion under PDPL.

## 10. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leakage: parties meet on platform then deal off it | High | High | Lead fee charged at response, not at close, so revenue does not depend on tracking the deal; escrow and contract make on-platform safer; reviews only for on-platform hires |
| Low-quality briefs drive agencies away | High | High | Human qualification in Year 1; budget band mandatory; phone OTP; refund lead fees on bad briefs; cap responders at 5 |
| Market too small | Medium | High | Adjacent categories in Year 2; regional expansion in Year 3; institutional demand partners |
| Entasher or a telco moves first | Medium | Medium | Founding-member exclusivity, verified reviews, local presence; be partner-ready for telcos and banks |
| Agency quality damages brand | Medium | High | Verification standard, references, probation for new agencies, remove on two bad reviews |
| PDPL non-compliance | Low | High | Consent capture, data map, deletion flow, DPO-equivalent role from day one |
| Escrow regulatory issue | Medium | Medium | Use licensed PSP split-payment product; legal opinion before Phase 3 |
| Founder dependency | High | Medium | Document processes; hire ops lead by Month 4 |

## 12. Exit and upside

Plausible acquirers in 3–5 years: regional marketplaces (Entasher, Sortlist), Jordanian telcos building SME ecosystems (Zain, Orange, Umniah), Gulf marketing holding groups, or classified/marketplace groups (OpenSooq, Dubizzle). The asset they buy is verified supply, pricing data, and transaction history, not the software.
